# Security Policy Framework Implementation Plan

## Overview
This academic governance case study develops a plan for introducing security policies at **United Medical Services**, a fictional healthcare organization incorporating a specialty medical clinic. The scenario centers on transitioning from a flatter organizational structure to a more hierarchical model while maintaining communication, employee engagement, and information security.

Rather than focusing on a single technical control, the project considers how security policies can be **published, communicated, taught, maintained, and improved** across an organization.

> **Project context:** This is a proposed implementation plan created for university coursework. The communication campaigns, security committee, training activities, and monitoring discussed below were **recommended**, not deployed or measured in a live healthcare environment.

## Objective
- Develop a practical approach for distributing organizational security policies.
- Clarify executive, Human Resources, and Information Security responsibilities.
- Design engaging employee security awareness activities.
- Recommend channels for communicating policy updates.
- Establish a recurring process for reviewing policies and incorporating employee feedback.

---

## Organizational Context
The assignment compared two common management structures:

| Structure | Governance considerations |
| --- | --- |
| Flat organization | More direct communication and collaboration, but responsibilities may be less formally defined |
| Hierarchical organization | Clearer reporting lines and responsibilities, but potentially slower communication across departments |

A successful policy rollout should preserve channels for employee questions and collaboration while creating clear accountability.

---

## Proposed Implementation Strategy

### 1. Centralize Policy Publication
- Maintain a centralized policy library accessible to on-site and remote employees.
- Organize policies by topic and provide a search function.
- Notify employees when policies are created, revised, or withdrawn.
- Offer downloadable, versioned PDF copies in addition to online access.
- Assign the **Information Security Manager** responsibility for policy maintenance and publication.

### 2. Communicate Policy Changes
The proposed communication plan combines:
- Organization-wide emails and policy change notifications
- Team and departmental meetings
- Mandatory training sessions
- Town hall discussions for major policy changes
- A form for employee questions, suggestions, and concerns

Human Resources and the Information Security Manager would review employee feedback and help resolve confusion about new requirements.

### 3. Coordinate Leadership and Human Resources
- Hold recurring meetings between Human Resources and executive management, with more frequent coordination during the transition.
- Have executives reinforce the importance of organizational security policies.
- Give Human Resources a central role in employee communication and disciplinary processes.
- Route questions and concerns to Human Resources and the Information Security Manager for review.

### 4. Deliver Interactive Awareness Training
The proposed training approach includes:
- Representatives from Information Security, Human Resources, and executive management
- Practical scenarios involving phishing and suspicious communications
- Exercises where employees work in teams to identify applicable policies and appropriate responses
- Instructions for reporting suspicious emails to the security team
- Downloadable training materials in an approved cloud folder
- Posters, reminders, and QR codes that help employees find guidance

The plan emphasizes that security is a shared organizational responsibility rather than something managed solely by IT.

---

## Continuous Communication

### Monthly Security Newsletter
The proposed newsletter would deliver short, accessible updates about:
- Important security policy changes
- Emerging threats and incident reminders
- Practical employee security tips
- Links to authoritative sources and updated documents

The Information Security Manager would validate technical accuracy, while Human Resources or communications staff would support distribution and readability.

### Login-Screen Security Reminders
Brief reminders could appear on workstations, email, electronic health record systems, VPN portals, and other frequently used systems. Suggested topics include password sharing, device locking, phishing, and incident reporting.

The project recommends avoiding excessive prompts that could create employee fatigue.

---

## Policy Maintenance & Feedback

### Proposed Review Committee
The assignment recommends a cross-functional committee involving technical and nontechnical staff. Its responsibilities would include:
1. Reviewing employee feedback and recurring policy questions.
2. Identifying policy requirements that create unintended operational difficulties.
3. Discussing security findings and compliance concerns raised by the IT security team.
4. Recommending updates that balance security needs with business operations.
5. Escalating urgent matters outside the normal meeting cycle.

The proposed committee would generally meet monthly, with additional meetings when risk or major changes justify them.

### Feedback Channels
Employees could submit feedback through Microsoft Forms, Qualtrics, email, surveys, or departmental meetings. Feedback should be taken seriously and, where possible, include suggestions for improving problematic policies.

### Illustrative Governance Cycle

```text
Publish policies
      |
      v
Communicate changes
      |
      v
Train employees
      |
      v
Collect feedback + review security findings
      |
      v
Evaluate and revise policies
      |
      +----------> Republish updates
```

This workflow is a conceptual design, not a deployed or automated system.

## Security Awareness Training Exercise
The coursework also developed an approximately **31-minute** sequence of public training videos addressing:

| Topic | Duration | Reason for inclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ransomware | 10:23 | Demonstrate the operational impact of malware, including healthcare disruption |
| Social media safety | 7:35 | Explain how oversharing can expose organizational information |
| Social engineering | 13:33 | Show how attackers manipulate individuals to gain access or information |

The plan recommends following videos with discussion, recognizing phishing indicators, and considering realistic employee scenarios rather than relying on passive viewing alone.

## Key Findings
- Security policy adoption requires clear communication and organizational ownership.
- Employee involvement can surface problems that a purely top-down rollout might overlook.
- Short, repeated reminders and interactive training can reinforce expectations.
- Regular policy review helps align controls with evolving business needs and risks.

## Lessons Learned
- Security governance depends on communication between IT, leadership, Human Resources, and employees.
- Policies need accessible publication, useful training, and transparent feedback processes.
- Organizational structure influences how effectively policies are understood and adopted.
- Compliance monitoring should inform policy improvement rather than operate in isolation.

## Conclusion
This case study presents a proposed organization-wide strategy for publishing, communicating, training, and maintaining security policies during a healthcare organizational transition. It demonstrates how cybersecurity governance extends beyond technical safeguards to include leadership alignment, employee education, feedback, and continuous improvement.
