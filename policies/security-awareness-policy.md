# Security Awareness & Training Policy

## Overview
This academic governance project develops a security awareness and training policy for **Bankwise Credit Union**, a fictional financial institution. It focuses on how employee education, clear responsibilities, and ongoing review can help protect sensitive financial information and reduce human-related security risks.

The project also evaluates security awareness training vendors and proposes questions for a request for information (RFI).

> **Project context:** University coursework based on a fictional case study. The policy and vendor evaluation questions are proposed deliverables; no production training program was deployed or vendor purchased.

## Objective
- Define employee security awareness responsibilities.
- Establish initial and annual training expectations.
- Address phishing, social engineering, privacy, and incident reporting.
- Identify policy ownership, training tracking, and governance responsibilities.
- Consider criteria for evaluating security awareness software providers.

---

## Security Challenges
- Employees may encounter phishing and social engineering attempts.
- Inconsistent training can increase the likelihood of mishandling customer information.
- Different employee roles require different levels of security training.
- Multiple branches may face training-delivery and tracking challenges.
- Organizational policies require routine review as threats and business practices change.

---

## Policy Design

### 1. Policy Statement
Bankwise Credit Union requires employees to complete security awareness training, protect customer information, follow organizational policies, and understand responsibilities associated with GLBA and other applicable security requirements.

### 2. Purpose
Help employees recognize common threats, use organizational systems responsibly, report suspicious behavior, and protect customer information.

### 3. Scope
Applies to employees, management, temporary workers, and contractors across all branches. Covered assets include workstations, mobile devices, email, networks, online banking services, and customer databases.

### 4. Training Requirements
- New employees complete security training before receiving access to sensitive customer information.
- Existing employees complete training annually.
- Training covers passwords, phishing, incident reporting, privacy, and acceptable use.
- Additional training is provided according to job responsibilities.
- Training completion is documented.

### 5. Responsibilities
| Role | Proposed responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Senior Management | Approve security policies |
| Information Security Officer | Oversee policy compliance |
| IT Department | Manage technical security controls and grant approved access |
| Human Resources | Track employee training completion |
| Department Managers | Ensure employees comply with requirements and approve access requests |
| Employees | Complete training and report suspicious activity |

To support separation of duties, management approves system access requests while IT provisions access.

### 6. Administrative Details
**Policy owner:** Information Security Officer  
**Approval authority:** Senior Management  
**Proposed version:** 1.0  
**Approval status:** Pending Approval  
**Review frequency:** Annually

## Security Education, Training & Awareness (SETA)
The research portion of the assignment highlights the need for a structured SETA program that:

1. Identifies training needs and objectives for different departments.
2. Provides both general and role-specific learning materials.
3. Uses accessible delivery channels, such as online instruction, email, newsletters, and posters.
4. Records training completion and gathers feedback.
5. Reassesses the program annually to address emerging threats and changing requirements.

The underlying principle is that technology alone cannot prevent every incident; employees also need understandable, recurring guidance.

---

## Implementation Considerations

### Multi-Branch Training
Online training can reduce disruption to customer service and make annual training available to geographically distributed employees.

### Employee Concerns
Restrictions on personal internet use and monitoring should be explained clearly, including their relationship to protecting customer data.

### Accountability
Managers and the Information Security Officer should review disputes related to responsibilities and separation of duties. Policy changes require senior management approval.

## Vendor Evaluation Exercise
The coursework identified three security awareness providers for further research:

| Vendor | Area considered in the assignment |
| --- | --- |
| KnowBe4 | Training content, phishing simulations, completion tracking |
| Hoxhunt | Interactive phishing simulations and adaptive employee training |
| Proofpoint | Security awareness training, simulations, and progress visibility |

**Example RFI questions developed for vendor evaluation:**
1. What training topics are provided?
2. Are phishing simulations included?
3. Can training be tailored by role or department?
4. How is completion tracked and reported?
5. What is the pricing structure and what fees are additional?
6. Does the platform address financial information protection?
7. How frequently is content updated?
8. What integrations are available for current email and IT systems?
9. What support is provided when technical issues occur?
10. Can training address risks involving online customer accounts and payment information?

The providers were **researched**, not implemented or formally scored.

## Key Findings
- Consistent security training helps reinforce expectations across an organization.
- Role-specific training can address risks that general awareness sessions may miss.
- Clear ownership and completion records improve accountability.
- Employee feedback and periodic review support ongoing program improvement.

## Lessons Learned
- Security awareness is a continuing program, not a one-time onboarding task.
- Policies become more actionable when they specify the people responsible for approval, delivery, tracking, and enforcement.
- Training programs should balance organizational security with employee usability.

## Conclusion
This project proposes a structured security awareness policy for a fictional credit union, covering training frequency, employee responsibilities, governance, continuous improvement, and vendor evaluation considerations. The case study demonstrates how policy and education can complement technical cybersecurity controls.
